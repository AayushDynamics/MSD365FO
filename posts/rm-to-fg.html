From Raw Materials to Finished Goods: A Comprehensive Journey in D365 F&O

Production control Published Aug 31, 2026 Aayush Tiwari 9 min read
▾Overview

In every manufacturing process, the transition from raw materials to finished goods involves critical stages, each contributing to operational and financial success. Here's a breakdown of the key events in this cycle and the corresponding accounting entries in D365 F&O.

▾1. Production planning

This stage sets the foundation for efficient resource allocation and scheduling: demand forecasting from historical data, Master Planning (MRP) to generate planned orders, and production order creation defining the BOM and routing. No financial entries post here, but these activities are vital for material reservation and readiness.

▾2. Production execution

Raw material and packing material picking list posting reserves and issues materials to production:

Accounting: Work In Progress (Estimated) — Dr.  |  Raw Material Inventory (Estimated) — Cr.  |  Packing Material Inventory (Estimated) — Cr.

Job/route card posting tracks labor hours, machine usage, and overhead costs:

Accounting: Work In Progress (Estimated) — Dr.  |  Labor/Machine Costs (Estimated) — Cr.

▾3. Report production output

Report the completed quantity against the production order, before the quality check stage, to reflect partially or fully completed production.

Report as finished — Accounting: Finished Goods estimated cost — Dr.  |  Work In Progress (Estimated) — Cr.

▾4. Quality control

In-process inspections test WIP inventory during production; finished goods quality checks happen before adding to inventory. No direct financial entries here, but this step is key to maintaining quality and reducing wastage.

▾5. Completion and costing

Production order ending marks the order as ended, transferring estimated cost to actual financial costs from WIP to finished goods.

Accounting (reversal of picking list, route cards & report-as-finished entries):

  • Raw Material Inventory — Cr.
  • Packing Material Inventory — Cr.
  • Labor/Machine Costs (Estimated) — Cr.
  • Work In Progress — Dr. and Cr.
  • Finished Goods Inventory — Dr.
