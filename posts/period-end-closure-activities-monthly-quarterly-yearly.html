Period End Closure Activities (Monthly/Quarterly/Yearly)

General ledger Published Sep 13, 2026 Aayush Tiwari 20 min
▾1. Executive overview

1.1 What "closing the period" actually means

The month-end close is the process of verifying and adjusting account balances at a period end so that the reports produced represent the company’s true financial position — fit to be put in front of management, investors, lenders, auditors and regulators.

In a pre-ERP world that was largely an accounting department exercise. In D365 it is not. Money and material are the same data. A goods receipt that the stores team never converted into an invoice is not a purchasing backlog item — it is a misstated inventory value and a misstated creditor balance. A production order that was reported as finished but never ended is not a shop-floor loose end — it is finished goods sitting in the balance sheet at the wrong cost. The close therefore reaches across purchasing, stores, production and sales, not just finance.

At the end of a fiscal year there is an additional, separate process: the year-end close, which transfers opening balances into the new year and moves profit and loss balances into retained earnings. That is covered in Section 9.

1.2 Why closes slip, and what D365 does about it

When an organisation moves onto an ERP platform, the most common complaint heard within the first year is that the finance period cannot be closed on time, which in turn delays the finalisation of the profit and loss account, the balance sheet and the audit. The root causes are consistent and they are organisational rather than technical:

  1. No properly defined process for period closing — it is carried in people’s heads.
  2. No defined roles and responsibilities at the level of individual tasks.
  3. No definite timelines, and no tracking against them.
  4. Communication gaps between departments, so finance discovers a blocker on day six that stores could have cleared on day one.

D365 answers all four with the Financial period close workspace: a configurable list of closing tasks, each with an owner, a task area, a relative due date, a direct link to the page where the work is done, and dependencies on other tasks. The workspace then tracks completion by company, by area and by person. Section 3 covers how to build it. Implementing it is, in practice, the single highest-return decision an organisation makes about its close.

1.3 The four-layer close model

Everything in this handbook hangs off one idea: the close is layered, and each layer must reconcile before the next one starts. Closing out of sequence is the single biggest cause of restated month-end numbers.

▾2. The close calendar

A close only compresses if the sequence is agreed in advance and the dependencies are respected. The timetable below is indicative — a business closing on day five will pull it tighter, a multi-plant manufacturer may need longer for costing — but the order is not negotiable, because each band depends on the one above it.

Figure 2 — Indicative period close calendar, from two days before period end to the period being locked

Two features of this calendar are worth drawing out, because they are the ones that most often get compressed and then cause rework.

  • Pre-close housekeeping starts before the period ends, not after. Chasing pending GRNs, gate entries without a material receipt, open purchase orders and undelivered sales orders on the last two days of the month removes most of the blockers that would otherwise surface on day three.
  • Foreign currency revaluation runs after everything in the sub-ledger is posted, and before reconciliation. Running it, early means running it again.
▾3. The Financial period close workspace

The Financial period close workspace helps you track closing processes across companies, areas and people. Depending on the view assigned to a user, they see either all tasks and statuses for a closing schedule, or only the tasks assigned to them. It is the governance layer that sits over everything else in this handbook.

3.1 How the configuration fits together

Configuration is built from the bottom up. Resources, task areas, closing roles, tasks and legal entities feed into a calendar and a template; the calendar and template are then tied to a specific period by a closing schedule, which is what appears in the workspace.

Closing roles define the type of responsibility that can be assigned within each area — for example preparer, reviewer, approver.

3.4 Step 3 — Resources

Resources answer the question "who does what". You map an employee to task areas and legal entities, and you set the view they get in the workspace. Two options exist, and the choice matters:

  • Only assigned tasks — the user sees only their own tasks. Users with this view cannot add, edit or remove tasks from the task list.
  • All tasks and status — the user sees every closing task and the status of the overall process. This is the financial controller’s view.

3.5 Step 4 — Calendar

The calendar defines the working days used for scheduling closing tasks, and therefore the target close date. If the intention is to close the month by the fifth working day of the following month, that is expressed here.

Create the calendar for a long horizon — a year or several years — because it can be edited afterwards. After creation, use Edit to mark specific days such as public holidays. Tasks are only scheduled on days where the Control value is Open; set it to Closed for a day on which no closing task should fall.

Path General ledger > Period close > Financial period close configuration > Calendars > New

Defining the period closure calendar and its working days

3.6 Step 5 — Template, task links and dependencies

The template defines every task that forms part of a closing process. A closing task is a recurring work effort assigned to an individual to complete each period — sub-ledger reconciliation, tax reporting, inventory closing, currency revaluation, depreciation posting, and so on.

For each task you define a relative due date: the number of days before or after the period end date that the task is due each period. You also assign a due time, set in the context of your own time zone, which converts to each user’s time zone automatically.

Path General ledger > Period close > Financial period close configuration > Templates > New

Defining tasks within a template, with relative due dates

Task navigation links

The task name in the workspace is a hyperlink to the page where the work is actually done. Setting this turns the workspace from a checklist into a working surface — the user clicks the task and lands on the right page. A currency revaluation task for Accounts payable, for example, links to the Foreign currency revaluation page. External URLs are also supported.

Path General ledger > Period close > Financial period close configuration > Templates > select task > Task link

Attaching a navigation link to a task

Linking a financial report to a task

To link a specific financial report to a close task, use the report URL. Open the report in the report designer, choose File > View report to open it in a browser, then copy the address bar contents into the task link URL field.

Dependencies

Tasks can depend on other tasks. Accounts payable cannot be closed, for example, until vendor foreign currency revaluation has run. A task that is blocked by an incomplete dependency shows a padlock icon in the workspace and cannot be marked complete.

Path General ledger > Period close > Financial period close configuration > Templates > select task > Set dependency

Setting dependencies between closing tasks

Use more than one template

Create separate templates for different period types — month end versus year end — or for groups of companies that close differently. Once one template exists, copy it to a new template and adjust. Only one template can be assigned to each closing schedule.

3.7 Step 6 — Closing schedule

The closing schedule ties everything together: it assigns a template to a specific financial period that must be closed, along with the calendar and the companies in scope. The tasks from the template are then generated automatically for that period, and the schedule appears in the workspace.

The Period end date on the schedule is what converts each task’s relative due date into an actual due date. If no calendar is assigned, due dates simply use all days of the week. Where a template task is assigned to multiple companies, D365 creates a separate task for each company in the schedule.

Path General ledger > Period close > Financial period close configuration > Closing schedules > New

Mapping task areas, calendar, template, resources and closing roles into a schedule

Path General ledger > Period close > All financial period close tasks

This list page holds current and previous period close tasks, with the scheduled due date, the actual completion date and the person who completed each task. It exports cleanly to Excel, which makes it the natural source both for measuring days-to-close and for demonstrating to auditors that the close followed a controlled, evidenced process.

Once a schedule is finished, set the Closed option on it. The task history stays available on the list page, but the schedule drops out of the workspace, and no further tasks can be added, edited or removed.

▾5. Month-end activities by module

Every department has work to do before the month can be closed. The activities below are grouped by D365 module rather than by department, because the same process is owned by different departments in different organisations. Adapt the ownership; keep the activities.

5.1 Accounts payable

Post invoices for all received purchase orders

Where material has been received against a purchase order and the MRN / GRN / product receipt has been posted, the invoice must be posted against that purchase order. Until it is, the vendor outstanding is understated and the financial inventory for those receipts has not been posted.

Path Accounts payable > Invoices > Pending vendor invoices

Post all pending invoices received from vendors

Where invoices have been received and entered but not posted, review them all before closing.

Path Accounts payable > Invoices > Vendor invoice journal

Post and settle all payments

Any vendor payment created but not posted must be dealt with before closing. Every open unsettled transaction should be settled so that the vendor statement and ageing are accurate.

Path Accounts payable > Payments > Vendor payment journal

Path Accounts payable > Vendors > All vendors > select vendor > Settle open transactions

Run foreign currency revaluation

Required wherever the company transacts in more than one currency. Run it only after all AP documents for the period are posted.

Path Accounts payable > Periodic tasks > Foreign currency revaluation

Reconcile vendor balances to the control accounts

Reconcile the vendor sub-ledger against the trial balance. Where there is a difference, check whether the trial balance voucher contains transactions with a posting type of Ledger, or any posting type other than vendor balance or vendor settlement — those do not post to the sub-ledger and are a common source of difference.

Path Accounts payable > Periodic tasks > Vendor to ledger reconciliation report

Path Accounts payable > Inquiries and reports > Vendor reports > Vendor balance list

Path General ledger > Inquiries and reports > Trial balance

Running the reconciliation report so it is useful

Provide the date range across which the variation is to be checked, select the posting profile if the two sources are being compared for different posting profiles, and tick both Include details and Differences only. Without those two check boxes the report is a balance listing rather than a reconciliation.

Set the fiscal period on hold for vendors

Always use On hold rather than Closed. A closed period cannot be reopened if an adjustment is later required.

Path General ledger > Calendars > Ledger calendars > select period > Period status

Further AP activities

  • All debit notes relating to short quantity should have been booked.
  • All MRNs should be made — there should be no pending MRN against a gate entry.
  • All purchase orders should be reviewed to verify actual pendency.
  • The effect of pending MRNs to be booked should be taken into provision.
  • Debit note and credit note reconciliation.
  • Purchase reconciliation against creditors.

One thing to remember about cost of goods sold

COGS is booked only for sales and sales return transactions. Any financial voucher, debit note or credit note raised against a customer that does not carry a quantity has no COGS impact at all. If margin looks wrong, check whether value-only credit notes are being used where a return order was needed.

  • Sales reconciliation against debtors.
  • Produce customer ageing after payments have been adjusted against pending invoices — not before.

5.3 Cash and bank

A bank reconciliation statement compares the cash balance in the company’s books with the corresponding amount on the bank statement. Reconciling the two identifies whether accounting changes are needed, and is one of the few controls that reliably detects both error and cash manipulation.

Path Cash and bank management > Bank accounts > Reconcile

Where advanced bank reconciliation is configured, import the bank statement first and then run the matching process.

Points to confirm

  • The balance per the bank statement reconciles to the bank balance in the system.
  • Bank charges have been booked as per the statement.
  • GST invoices raised by the bank have been accounted for.

Why the two balances differ

The bank’s closing balance and the company’s closing balance are almost always different. The usual reasons:

Reason

What it is

Deposits in transit

Cash and cheques received and recorded by the company but not yet recorded on the bank statement.

Outstanding cheques

Cheques issued to creditors where the payment has not yet been processed by the bank.

Bank service fees

Charges the bank deducts for services provided, usually small individually.

Interest income

Interest credited by the bank on certain accounts.

Not sufficient funds (NSF) cheques

A cheque deposited by a customer where the issuer’s account lacked funds. The bank deducts the previously credited amount and returns the cheque to the depositor.

Bank charges and GST — a practical workaround

The bank is an internal party in D365, so the system does not calculate or post tax on bank transactions directly. The common solution is to create each bank as a vendor, grouped to a single main account, and route charge transactions through that vendor account with GST, paying it off afterwards. The charge voucher becomes: bank charges Dr, GST recoverable Dr, bank vendor Cr; and the payment voucher: bank vendor Dr, bank Cr.

5.5 Inventory

The governing principle for inventory at month end is simple: make sure all physical inventory is posted as financial — delivered, received, or reported as finished. Physical-only transactions carry estimated cost, and estimated cost is what produces an inventory value that does not agree with the ledger.

This section lists the month-end actions. The mechanics of the inventory close itself — pre-closing, the two check reports, the close and the reconciliation — are covered in full in Section 6.

Post all required inventory adjustment journals

Path Inventory management > Journal entries > Items

Post inventory counting

Path Inventory management > Journal entries > Item counting > Counting

Close in-transit transfer orders wherever possible

Review all transfer orders with a shipped status and receive everything that can legitimately be received. Anything left sits in goods in transit on the balance sheet.

Path Inventory management > Outbound orders > Transfer orders (filter on Shipped)

Perform inventory adjustments where required

This is for correcting wrongly posted cost on purchase transactions — for instance where 500 per unit was posted instead of 50, an adjustment of 450 is passed against the transaction.

Path Inventory management > Periodic tasks > Closing and adjustment > Adjustment

Run inventory recalculation or inventory closing

Recalculation derives the final valuation and produces the FIFO or LIFO valuation — remember that during the month D365 posts at a running weighted average even where the item model group specifies FIFO or LIFO. Closing additionally settles receipts against issues and locks the period. See Section 6.7 for which to use.

Path Inventory management > Periodic tasks > Closing and adjustment > Recalculation / Close procedure > Close

Reconcile inventory to the trial balance

Match the inventory balance to the trial balance. Where they differ, check for ledger-type transactions posted directly to inventory accounts, and for any item group change made during the month.

5.6 Production

Post picking lists and routes for the current month’s production

Path Production control > Production orders > All production orders

Post adjustments where required

To adjust a posted picking list, report as finished, or route card.

Report as finished

Reporting as finished is what gives you the inventory that has actually been produced and is physically available, at the right cost.

End production orders for the current month

Every production order that has been fully reported as finished must be ended. Ending is what produces the final cost of the finished goods and the financially closed inventory. An order left un-ended carries its cost outside the trial balance.

Path Production control > Production orders > All production orders (Process > End)

Further production activities

  • All production orders should be reviewed; the Balance production order report is useful for this.
  • Production orders that have been started should be completed in the system at month end.
  • Rejection of material at the shop floor should be punched into the system.
  • Inventory taking of all material at the shop floor, including inventory in shop-floor warehouses and material in excess warehouses.

Sequence matters when production cascades

Where a production cycle involves multiple production orders, ending a later order before the earlier one produces an incorrect cost. Orders must be ended in the sequence of the production cycle.

A manufactured product must never be used as an input to the same production order — the cost flow will be wrong.

▾6. Inventory period close in detail

Inventory period closure calculates the costing for the period and closes inventory transactions up to the defined date. It is the layer that most often delays a close, and the layer where mistakes are most expensive, because a bad inventory close contaminates cost of goods sold, gross margin, the balance sheet and every subsequent month until it is unwound.

The inventory close process settles issue transactions against receipt transactions, based on the inventory valuation method selected in the item’s item model group. As part of that settlement you can specify that the general ledger be updated to reflect the adjustments made. Until inventory close or recalculation has been run, issue transactions are posted at the calculated running average cost price — whatever the item model group says.

What inventory close locks

After inventory close, you can no longer post in periods before the inventory closing date you set, unless you reverse the completed inventory close. If inventory close is run for the period ending 31 January, you cannot post transactions dated earlier than 31 January.

Inventory close is a required step in the month-end closing procedure for all inventory models except moving average. D365 will warn you if you try to close a financial period without first performing the inventory close as of the period end date.

How often inventory close runs varies by company, but transaction volume should drive the decision. In general, most companies run inventory close as part of their month-end close and reconciliation procedures. Microsoft recommends running it during off-peak hours so that computing resources are spread more evenly.

6.7 Recalculation or close — which one

The two are not interchangeable, and the difference is about whether the period stays open.

Figure 6 — Choosing between inventory recalculation and inventory close

Inventory recalculation adjusts on-hand inventory and inventory transactions but does not make settlements. Both processes affect whichever ledger account was linked to the original inventory transaction — which has an important consequence:

Adjustments follow the original accounts, not the current ones

When a purchase order is created from a sales order, the general ledger accounts used for the original sales order are the ones updated. Even if the ledger accounts for the item group assigned to the item were changed after the sales order was posted, and a recalculation created an adjustment amount, that adjustment posts to the original ledger accounts — not to the new accounts now assigned to the item.

This is the mechanical reason why changing item groups or inventory posting profiles mid-year creates reconciliation differences that never resolve themselves. See Section 11.

To review the ledger voucher posted by either task: on the Closing and adjustment page, on the Overview tab, select the update to review, then select Details > Voucher.

Run month by month — both recalculation and close

If recalculation or closing is run for October, November and December together, the adjustments for October and November are posted in December. The October and November trial balances will then never match the inventory value report, and the entire impact lands in December.

Always run one period at a time, in date order, and always in batch.

6.9 The inventory close log and its warnings

After the close completes, a message may report that a unit cost price might be incorrect because a transaction could not be fully settled. Before that message appears, the system reports the item number and the affected transaction, and explains that the cost amount used was not updated by the close. The message appears when an issue-type transaction cannot be settled.

During the close, the system checks each financial dimension to see whether there are more issues than receipts up to the specified closing date. That imbalance typically arises when an inventory transaction from a purchase order is not fully posted financially — either the vendor invoice has not been received, or bill of materials components included in a higher-level production are not financially posted because the sub-production has not been cost-calculated. In that case the close cannot adjust all issues to the correct cost price, because there is not enough receipt information available.

Where the log produces many warnings, the recommended actions are:

  • Update receipts financially — post the outstanding vendor invoices.
  • Advance the closing date.
  • Re-evaluate the business procedures that are producing the gap.

In some circumstances nothing can be done. If marking is used and the marked purchase order has a financial date after the closing date, the closing date cannot be changed.

6.10 Reversing a completed inventory close

Occasionally a completed inventory close has to be reversed to return settlements to the state they were in before adjustments were made. Reversing reopens inventory for posting in the period the close covered, and related changes may also be made in the general ledger. Once the adjustments are finished, run inventory close again for that period.

Treat reversal as an exception, not a routine

A reversal is a legitimate tool when a genuine error is found before reporting. It is not a substitute for getting the pre-close checks right, and every reversal should carry a documented reason — auditors will ask why a closed period was reopened.

▾11. Do’s and don’ts

Everything in this section comes from differences that actually arose and had to be rectified. There is a common thread running through almost all of them: master data that is changed part-way through a financial year does not retrospectively change what has already been posted, so the sub-ledger and the ledger diverge and stay diverged until somebody passes a journal.

The single rule behind most of this section

Changing a vendor group, a customer group, an item group, or a main account in a posting profile during a live financial year will create a reconciliation difference. If the change is unavoidable, work out the affected balance first and post the transfer journal at the same time — not at year end when nobody remembers why.

11.1 Accounts payable

DO

  • Use Match product receipt to change the GRN quantity on a purchase invoice.
  • Follow legacy code nomenclature for vendors. Where a new nomenclature is needed, use the external code option (Entity name – External codes for vendors).
  • Process purchase returns through the credit note functionality.
  • Use the Is small business field and Registration ID to hold MSME status, with separate terms of payment carrying MSME in the code, for identification and to meet the 45-day payment requirement.
  • Maintain terms of payment on the vendor master so that due dates — invoice date plus terms — and vendor ageing are meaningful.
  • Enable three-way matching, and set the post-invoice-with-discrepancies policy to Require approval so a senior person approves discrepancies before the AP clerk posts.
  • Set Require document date on vendor invoice to Error, which also prevents a document date later than the posting date.
  • Block the vendor balance ledger account for manual entries.
  • Where a vendor supplies both goods and services and they must be booked separately, create two vendors so that two vendor groups can be attached.

DON'T

  • Do not change the vendor group of a vendor during an ongoing financial year.
  • Do not change the item group on an item — it changes the main account mapping for its purchases.
  • Do not open MSME as a vendor group; MSME status can change.
  • Do not change the quantity manually on a purchase invoice instead of using Match product receipt — it causes a mismatch in the purchase accrual balance and under- or over-costing of inventory.
  • Do not post multi-vendor accounting through a single voucher. It breaks vendor to ledger reconciliation, and the accounting source explorer and voucher transaction reports show only the first vendor account.

If a change has already been made

  • If a vendor group was changed mid-year, post a journal voucher crediting the balance of the original summary account to the new main account used.
  • If an item group needs to be changed, post the corresponding journal voucher.
  • Maintain a separate account for manual adjustment entries to vendor balances, used only for year-end adjustments.
  • Where expenses are loaded onto inventory after the material is booked, a single ledger account is credited as expense with the offset being the inventory movement, per the item posting profile Profit/Loss posting types. If separate ledger accounts are needed, add a charge code on the purchase order for the service item being loaded and configure it — the item adjustment account posted through the posting profile is then nullified.

11.2 Accounts receivable

DO

  • Process sales returns through the credit note functionality.
  • Finalise profitability analysis dimensions — customer segment, brand and similar — in advance, and configure them in the account structure or an advanced rule.
  • Maintain profitability analysis dimensions properly on the customer and item masters. If a mandatory dimension is missing, the system will not allow the sales order to post.
  • Run the Customer to ledger reconciliation report with Include details and Differences only whenever the customer balance and the summary account disagree.

DON'T

  • Do not change the customer group on a customer — it causes a mismatch between the customer balance and the attached summary account.
  • Do not change the item group on an item; it changes the main account mapping for its sales.
  • Do not post directly to the customer ledger account.

If a change has already been made

  • Where a customer group was changed, use a journal voucher to debit the existing balance of the original summary account to the new main account associated with the changed group.
  • Where an item group was changed on an item, post a journal voucher to reconcile sales and the other ledger accounts associated with the original item group.
  • Maintain a separate account for manual adjustments to customer balances, used only for year-end adjustments.

11.3 Stock transfer

DO

  • Refer to the Stock transfer shipped but not received report to analyse pending shipments. Timely booking of transfer receipts is a primary requirement for the balance sheet.
  • Work out the value of on-hand stock financially updated, using the inventory value report, before changing an item group or a posting profile — then transfer the value by journal from the old main account to the new one.
  • Track goods in transit and interim transit branch-wise from the stock transfer shipment and receipt registers. These are asset accounts that accumulate over time, and D365 does not give you a unit-wise breakdown of who owes what.

DON'T

  • Do not change the item group on an item without proper reconciliation.
  • Do not change the inventory posting profile for transfer orders without proper reconciliation and rectification.

11.5 Inventory valuation

  1. Where the setup in the item model group is changed for some items, the stock sheet shows inventory valuation per the updated item model group, but inventory that has already flowed into the financial ledger does not change retrospectively — creating a mismatch to that extent.
  2. If Include physical value is unticked in the item model group, the value received on the GRN does not flow into the expected cost for valuation purposes, creating differences in inventory valuation.
  3. If Post financial entry is unticked, financial entries are not posted even though physical stock is negated, creating a difference between the physical stock sheet and the financial stock.
  4. Production orders for which a picking list or report as finished has been posted must be ended, and reviewed periodically at a frequency management decides. If they are not ended on time, the financial cost of inventory does not flow to the trial balance.
  5. Purchase orders for which a GRN has been posted must be invoiced, and reviewed periodically. If they are not invoiced, financial cost does not flow and inventory valuation is wrong.
  6. Inventory closing is to be processed monthly, with the closing date in the following month decided by management. After the inventory period is closed, no entry related to inventory can be posted in that period.
  7. Costing of positive inventory adjustments depends on the method: under FIFO or weighted average the user must define the unit price manually; under moving average the system picks the running average automatically; under standard cost the system picks the predefined standard cost.

Other item model group behaviours worth knowing

If Accrue liability on product receipt is ticked, a financial voucher is posted at GRN and automatically reversed when the purchase invoice is posted. If unticked, no financial voucher is posted at GRN.

Post product receipt in ledger in Accounts payable parameters (Update tab) must be ticked alongside Accrue liability on product receipt in the item model group to enable financial posting of the GRN.

If Stocked product is unticked, inventory for that item is not maintained and all purchases are booked directly to profit and loss instead of inventory accounts.

If Post to financial inventory is unticked, COGS is not posted to the trial balance.

If the main account for posting type Discount is not linked and not tracked separately, discount is absorbed into the revenue account in the trial balance. Linking it means the unit price is the inventory cost and the discount is booked in P&L without affecting inventory cost.

If Update vendor accounting using the invoice date is ticked in Accounts payable parameters (Invoice tab), the posting date for invoice booking becomes the vendor invoice date rather than the actual posting date.

▾13. Frequently asked questions

13.1 Opening balances and cut-over

Note – A detailed Cut over discussion will be scheduled with respective stakeholders before go-live.

We are going live mid-year. Inventory valuation is not finalised on the cut-off date. Can we upload quantities now and values later?

You can, but it costs you. If opening quantities are uploaded without value, or at a tentative value, all issues from those quantities flow into costing at that value. When the actual values are loaded later, every issue has to be corrected by manually selecting the price — a tedious exercise given the volumes, and one that continues until the opening stock is exhausted. If the issues are not corrected manually, inventory costing will differ from actual cost and the valuation will be wrong.

The better approach is to finalise the inventory valuation for the cut-off date as quickly as possible and upload quantity and value together. An organisation moving to D365 on 1 April can typically finalise closing inventory valuation within five days and then load both. Continuing to operate in the legacy software during those five days is not advisable; once error-free opening stock with value is loaded, the five days of transactions can be recorded in D365 as well.

The alternative is to pass a financial entry directly in the books, but such an entry will invite an audit observation and will need explaining.

The trade-off, stated plainly

The longer it takes to finalise inventory valuation, the more certain it is that either inventory costing will differ from actual cost, or somebody will spend weeks feeding values manually.

We need to change the inventory valuation method — say FIFO to weighted average. What happens?

There are mismatches in inventory costing when the method changes. The remedy is the same as at cut-over: close the inventories with value on the cut-off date so that costing is error-free from the date of change onwards. Use Inventory management > Periodic > Closing and adjustment > Close procedure > Close, fill in the closing date, set the specification to Total or Item number, and select OK.

In practice these changes are rare in running operations, and are generally neither recommended nor accepted by auditors unless there is a valid reason for the change.

13.2 Costing and returns

Physical inventory was issued at expected cost and billed, and now a sales return has arrived. Why is the cost wrong?

Where physical inventory is issued at expected cost — which happens when the actual invoice has not been booked by then — and the finished inventory is invoiced before the purchase invoice and the loading of other expenses, the costing mismatches actual cost. It is further misstated when a sales return makes the physical quantity positive again without picking up a value. The same problem occurs when production orders are closed but the physical finished goods were invoiced at expected cost, and it intensifies when a sales return then lands on those invoices.

Book all purchase invoices and all expense loading onto inventory so that costing is accurate. When physical quantity is received back through a sales return, pick the right COGS by selecting the batch, or by entering the value manually. Where the sales invoice with the particular batch is not selected on the return, fill in the return cost price manually at Sales order > Line details > Setup tab > Return cost price.

What happens when sales and purchase returns are not mapped to the original invoice?

In D365, receipt values are set by the user while issue costs are calculated by the system. A sales return is a receipt of goods, so the right cost has to be picked for correct valuation. On a purchase return, if the original purchase invoice is not mapped, the system derives a value from LIFO, FIFO or whatever valuation method applies. Both sales returns and purchase returns should be mapped to the original invoices against which the return takes place.

Why does inventory in transit come back at a different cost?

When material is in transit between branches, the receiving branch books the goods at an average price of all the in-transit batches. If location A sends batch 1 at 10 and batch 2 at 11, location B receives both at 10.50. That creates a mismatch between the actual in-transit inventory value and the received value at B. This is the base product behaviour; a customisation exists that books inventory at the receiving location based on the order of the in-transit order from the originating location.

COGS is booked in the trial balance. How do we prepare a balance sheet from it?

Use the identity: COGS = consumption of RM/PM + purchases of finished and traded goods + opening of FG, WIP and traded − closing of FG, WIP and traded. The balance sheet can be prepared from that equation.

13.3 Year-end close

Why can I not see previous year closings in the History section of the Year-end close page?

The system maintains subsequent year-end close process information. Vouchers from all year-end close processes can be viewed on the Voucher transactions page.

The year-end close fails because the reporting currency does not balance. What does that mean?

This can occur after enabling the Awareness between ledger settlement and year-end close feature. With it enabled, settled ledger transactions are excluded from the opening balance of the next year. Ledger settlement is performed only for the accounting currency, and validation only ensures that accounting-currency debits equal credits — the reporting currency amounts for those transactions are not validated and may not balance. Ledger settlement also does not automatically calculate and post a gain or loss in the reporting currency. A gain or loss transaction must therefore exist in the reporting currency when performing ledger settlement; if it is not included, the year-end close reports an out-of-balance.

Why is ledger settlement automation not processing December’s transactions?

The Automate ledger settlements feature runs for transactions dated from the first day of the fiscal year to the current date when the occurrence runs. For fiscal years ending 31 December, an automation scheduled for the first of every month runs on 1 December and next on 1 January — so transactions dated 2 to 31 December are never considered. Change the January occurrence to run on 31 December instead.

Where do the year-end close templates live now?

With the General ledger year-end enhancements feature enabled, template setup moved to its own page, Year-end close template setup, and is separated from running the close. The Year-end close page then behaves like the General ledger foreign currency revaluation page, listing each time the close was run or reversed, with an accounting manager initiating the run from there.

Keeping this document current

Microsoft ships changes to the year-end close roughly every release. Two areas in particular have moved recently and are worth re-checking before each year end: the separation of the Year-end close template setup page from the close itself, and the Validate year-end close checks, which gained additional partial-settlement tests in version 10.0.45. Confirm against your current environment version before a year end rather than after it.
